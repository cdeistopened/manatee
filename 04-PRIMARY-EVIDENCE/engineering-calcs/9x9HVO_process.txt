                                        The Process

Biomass is grown in a 2-tank system where abundant light, nutrients, CO2 and warmth
are provided. Periodically a pump-venturi pulls biomass and water from the bottom of
one tank and transfers it to another, aerating it with CO2 via the venturi. This raises the
level of one tank and concentrates biomass on the surface. This overflows back to the
other tank. Over time the concentration of biomass in one tank becomes more than 6%
by weight. A light sensor indicates when it is dense enough.

It is then transferred to a closed tank where it will be processed. Inside this tank it is
drawn in to a high pressure (300psi) pump and ejected through a unique, ultra-cavitating
nozzle. Inside the nozzle a portion of the biomass and water (3%) are converted into
hydrocarbons via a process called hydrous pyrolysis. The stream is re-injected into the
processing tank where the mass converted to hydrocarbons floats at the surface while the
remainder sinks into the tank and is sent through the nozzle again.

Over time a layer of hydrocarbons forms on the surface. When fresh biomass is injected
into the tank, the water level rises. This displaces most of the hydrocarbons into a
collection bladder. A “S” shaped tube with a sub-surface inlet returns a portion of the
tank water, containing residual chlorophyl, back into the greenhouse tanks where it is re-
used in future generations of algae.

The hydrocarbons accumulate in the storage bladder. They stratify, separating any
residual water. They are off-loaded when the robot returns to port. Periodically, the robot
directs some biomass into a closed but not sealed tank. This is allowed to decompose,
generating CO2 for growth. The waste is returned to the greenhouse tanks.
