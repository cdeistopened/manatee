## 1. The Historical Bottleneck: The Energy Cost of Separation

In industrial microalgae processing, water typically contains only 0.5 to 1.0 grams of dry biomass per liter (0.05% to 0.1% solids). Removing 999 grams of water to recover 1 gram of microalgae using high-speed disc-stack centrifuges requires **1.5 to 4.0 kWh per cubic meter** of water processed.

$$\text{Energy per kg dry algae} = \frac{\text{Specific Pumping/Centrifuge Energy (kWh/m}^3\text{)}}{\text{Algae Concentration (kg/m}^3\text{)}} = \frac{2.5 \text{ kWh/m}^3}{0.001 \text{ kg/m}^3} = 2,500 \text{ kWh/kg}$$

This massive energy barrier means traditional microalgae facilities consume more energy harvesting the cells than the cells contain in chemical fuel energy (EROI < 1).
