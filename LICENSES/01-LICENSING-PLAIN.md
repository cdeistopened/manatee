# What You Can Do with the Manatee

The Manatee — hardware, docs, and firmware — is open source. Here's what that means in practice, no lawyer required.

## Build it. Sell it. No permission needed.

You can build a Manatee for yourself, modify it, and even sell finished units or kits — commercially, at scale, without asking us first and without paying a royalty. That's the deal. Nobody at Solaris Cybernetics needs to approve your business plan.

## If you improve the hardware, share it back

The one condition: if you change the hardware design — a better hull, a smarter mount, a cheaper drivetrain — and you distribute that change (sell it, publish it, hand it to a customer), you have to release your modified design files under the same license, CERN-OHL-S v2. You can't take the commons, improve it, and lock the improvement behind a patent or a closed spec sheet. Every fork feeds the fleet.

This only kicks in when you *distribute*. Tinkering in your own garage and never sharing it triggers nothing.

## The docs are yours to copy, remix, translate

The manuals, plans, and guides are under CC BY-SA 4.0. Copy them, translate them, rewrite them for your local conditions — just credit the Manatee Project and share your version under the same license. Same spirit as the hardware: the writing stays in the commons too.

## Firmware is MIT — do anything, just keep the notice

Any code that runs on the Manatee (sensors, motor control, telemetry) is MIT licensed. That's the loosest license in this package: use it, modify it, bundle it into a closed product, whatever you want. The only obligation is to keep the copyright notice attached.

## Why reciprocal, not permissive, for the hardware

We picked CERN-OHL-S — the "strong" reciprocal variant — specifically so the design can't be enclosed. A permissive license would let someone take the raft, bolt on one proprietary part, and sell it as a closed system with no way back into the commons. Reciprocal licensing closes that door: improve it, and the improvement comes home.

## No patents here

The underlying principles — algae remediation, solar-driven filtration — rest on expired or public-domain technology. There is no patent monopoly protecting the Manatee, and none of this is "patented" anything. What these three licenses govern is narrower and more concrete: the specific design files, drawings, documentation, and code we publish. Build on the ideas freely; the license just asks that if you build on *our files*, you play by the same rules we did.
